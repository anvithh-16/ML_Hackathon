Generated by `python -m src.eval.compare_models` -> `results/model_comparison.csv`. Every model went through the same
runner (`src/eval/cv_runner.py`): same 78 training trips, same 5 group-aware folds (`configs/folds.json`), same
subsample (every 3rd observation), same metrics. student_test (10 trips, 18 631 labelled observations) is used only
for comparison, never for training or tuning.

## Primary selection metric and why
Order of criteria, fixed before the final runs:
1. **Observation-level Top-1 accuracy on student_test**, because it matches the map-matching objective (right arc for each GPS point).
2. **CV stability** (Macro F1 mean and std over the 5 folds).
3. **Probability quality (log loss)**, because the HMM uses the probabilities as emissions.
4. **Inference time.**

## 3.3 Figures (`results/figures/`)
- `cv_macro_f1.png` — all models except the balanced Logistic Regression sit at 0.89–0.92 and the error bars overlap, so CV
  alone cannot separate Decision Tree, Random Forest and XGBoost. The balanced Logistic Regression is lowest (0.817)
  because class weighting lowers precision at the 0.5 threshold.
- `test_top1_macro_f1.png` — Top-1 ranges from 0.832 (KNN) to 0.862 (XGBoost default), a narrow band. Macro F1 and Top-1 do not
  always agree (balanced Logistic Regression has a slightly higher Top-1 than the default but by far the lowest Macro F1), which is why
  Top-1 is the primary metric.
- `test_logloss.png` — ensembles of trees give the best probabilities (about 0.07); the single Decision Tree and KNN are
  coarser (0.080 and 0.113) because their probabilities are leaf frequencies or vote fractions.
- `inference_time.png` (log scale) — KNN needs 368 ms per 1 000 rows, about 800 times XGBoost (0.47 ms), because it searches
  its stored training rows at prediction time. Random Forest is about 4× slower than XGBoost.
- `confusion_<model>.png` — for XGBoost default on student_test: 15 487 true positives, 3 143 missed, 2 158 false alarms
  among 195 635 candidate rows. KNN has more of both error types (3 929 and 2 624).

## Model selected and justification
**Selected: XGBoost with default parameters (`xgb__default`).** Exported to `artifacts/final/` (manifest:
`artifacts/final/manifest.json`).

1. **Top-1:** highest of all models, 0.862 against 0.858 for tuned XGBoost, 0.856 for Random Forest and 0.540 for the
   nearest-candidate baseline.
2. **CV stability:** Macro F1 0.9125 ± 0.0235, indistinguishable from Random Forest (0.9129 ± 0.0226) given the fold spread.
3. **Probability quality:** the lowest log loss (0.0692), which is what the HMM consumes.
4. **Speed:** 0.47 ms per 1 000 rows, about 4× faster than Random Forest.

**Why the tuned model was not chosen.** Bayesian optimisation (Optuna TPE, 40 trials, `results/tuning/xgb_trials.csv`)
raised CV Macro F1 only from 0.9125 to 0.9151 (+0.0026, smaller than the fold standard deviation of about 0.022) and
did not improve student_test Top-1 (0.858 vs 0.862). The tuned model is also twice as slow (0.92 vs 0.47 ms per 1 000 rows).
Tuned and untuned were compared on identical folds and partitions (`results/tuning/tuned_vs_untuned.csv`), so we keep
the simpler model, as decided before tuning.

**A caveat we report openly.** With the HMM on top (`results/hmm/clf_vs_hmm.csv`), Random Forest scores reach a slightly
higher Top-1 than XGBoost (0.951 vs 0.942), while XGBoost has the best route continuity of the five (0.979), no
unmatched observations and far fewer route breaks (0.2 vs 1.8 per trip on average). The difference comes from 10 test trips and is within the
trip-to-trip noise, so we did not change the choice that the pre-declared criteria produced. The HMM is the larger
effect: it raises XGBoost's Top-1 from 0.880 (classifier-only) to 0.942 and route continuity from 0.935 to 0.979.
